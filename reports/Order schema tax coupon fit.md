# Order tables record taxes well but fail refunds

**The current order schema fits checkout-time capture but does not fit the full scenario.** It stores the amounts for multiple stacked coupons, per-product tax rates, a separate shipping tax and EU per-product shipping tax correctly at the moment an order is created. It fails on everything that happens afterwards: partial refunds, order edits, exact audit of the net bases, and tax-inclusive reporting. The most serious gap is the `refunds` table, which holds one money amount and no link to items, taxes, coupons or shipping. Close behind are the missing net bases (net shipping, shipping discount, per-line allocated shipping, per-line taxable base), the rebuild-on-edit behaviour that rewrites historical tax and coupon rows from today's settings, and the mixed gross/net bases under tax-inclusive pricing. Several further problems are behavioural rather than structural (an unused `charge_taxes` flag, a hard-coded shipping-tax toggle, EU shipping that cannot carry an independent rate), and the notes flag a few items as unverified. Every code claim below comes from reading the repository; no tests or runtime checks were run. Claims about EU law and other platforms come from secondary or vendor sources unless stated, and the notes found no primary national text for the proportional shipping split.

## What the schema stores today

Seven tables hold order data: `orders`, `order_items`, `order_taxes`, `order_coupons`, `order_item_coupons`, `refunds` and `order_activities`. Every amount is an integer in minor units, stored as an `invoiced_*` (order currency) and `base_*` (store currency) pair; `base_*` is authoritative and `invoiced_*` is converted column by column (code: `database/migrations/Create*Table.php`, `CreateOrderAction.php`). An order carries subtotal, shipping total (net of shipping discount, plus shipping tax), discount total (items plus shipping discount), tax total, shipping tax amount, total, and an order-level `is_tax_inclusive` flag. An item carries unit price, regular price, discount, tax total, subtotal and total, with no rate column by design (code: `openspec/specs/order-tax-lines/spec.md`).

Tax detail lives in `order_taxes`: one row per tax component and scope, holding `type` (product or shipping), a free-text `name`, a `rate` of decimal(8,4), the amounts, and a nullable `order_item_id`. This representation handles three of your four requirements. Items at 0%, 7% and 19% in one order produce three rows with their own rates, because both shipped strategies compute a rate per cart line (code: `EUTaxStrategy.php:29-31`, `PersistsOrderTaxes.php:59-65`). A separate shipping tax rate works for non-EU destinations: `DefaultTaxStrategy` resolves a dedicated shipping rate and writes one `shipping` row with a null item (code: `DefaultTaxStrategy.php:69-84`). The EU distribution works too: `EUTaxStrategy` allocates the post-discount shipping fee across lines in proportion to each line's taxable amount using largest-remainder allocation, taxes each portion at that line's rate with HALF_UP rounding, and writes one `shipping` row per line with `order_item_id` set (code: `EUTaxStrategy.php:96-141`). That approach matches the commonly described practice for mixed-rate baskets, where shipping follows the goods and is split by value; a German advisory example splits 6 of shipping across a 10 net book at 7% and a 20 net item at 19% as 2 and 4 ([Taxdoo](https://www.taxdoo.com/?p=7795)). Note the evidence weight: this is vendor and advisory material, and Ireland's Revenue only states the general rule that multiple supplies are apportioned and composite supplies follow the principal element ([Revenue.ie](https://revenue.ie/en/vat/charging-vat/vat-and-mixed-supplies-of-goods/index.aspx)). The notes found no German, Austrian or Dutch primary text confirming the pro-rata method is mandatory, and forum sources describe "majority by value" and fixed-rate simplifications too ([Shopware forum](https://forum.shopware.com/t/versandkosten-steuersatz-problem-fuer-de-bei-gemischten-artikeln-im-wk-mit-mwst-7-und-19/101171)).

Coupons also fit well. `order_coupons` holds one frozen row per coupon (code, title, type, target, a full JSON rule snapshot, total discount in both currencies, `usage_reversed_at`), and `order_item_coupons` holds the per-line share of each coupon. Two product coupons on one line produce two attribution rows; an order-wide coupon is split across lines with largest-remainder so shares sum exactly; product coupons run first, then order-wide coupons run against the remainder; the stored discount is clamped to the line (code: `DiscountService.php:347-375, 431-472, 595-641`, `PersistsOrderCoupons.php:102-123`). Free-shipping coupons store only a waived total on `order_coupons` with no item rows. Discount is applied before tax: the taxable base is the line amount minus the line discount (code: `RecalculateCartAction.php:294-300`), which is the correct direction for retailer-funded discounts, since CJEU case law lets a price reduction lower the taxable amount ([HMRC on Elida Gibbs and Kuwait Petroleum](https://gov.uk/hmrc-internal-manuals/vat-supply-and-consideration/vatsc06640)).

## The refund model cannot reverse what the order recorded

This is the most severe gap, and it is structural. `refunds` has `order_id`, `status`, `invoiced_amount`, `reason`, `refund_type`, the gateway `refund_id` and audit columns (code: `CreateRefundsTable.php:27-38`). It has no `base_amount`, no refund-item table, no quantity, no tax split, no shipping share and no coupon link. Three consequences follow. First, a partial refund of one discounted item that carries its share of shipping tax cannot be expressed: the amount is a lump, and nothing connects it to the matching `order_taxes` rows, so a credit note with correct per-rate VAT reversal cannot be produced without recomputation. The Art. 226 invoice content rule requires taxable amount, rate and VAT per rate ([legislation.gov.uk](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/eudr/2006/112/article/226)), and reductions after the sale reduce the taxable amount under the Elida Gibbs line of cases ([HMRC](https://gov.uk/hmrc-internal-manuals/vat-supply-and-consideration/vatsc06640)); the notes did not retrieve Arts. 90 and 219 on credit notes, so treat the credit-note framing as design inference. Second, the refundable cap in `CreateRefundAction` subtracts `invoiced_shipping_total`, so shipping and its tax are never refundable through this action, and the file carries a `@todo` (code: `CreateRefundAction.php:134`). Third, `mark_refund_as_completed` sets the whole order to `REFUNDED` even when the refund is partial (code: `OrderService.php:~395-420`, line numbers approximate). Coupon usage is reversed only on cancellation, never on refund (code: `OrderManager.php:148-150`). Quantity-level returns also have no rule: attributions are per line for all units, with no per-unit share or remainder policy.

The suggested structure is a refund-line table, modelled on how the reference platforms work. Magento keeps refunded counters per order item for amount, tax and discount, and Shopify exposes post-refund shipping prices ([Magento db_schema.xml](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/magento/magento2/2.4-develop/app/code/Magento/Sales/etc/db_schema.xml), [Shopify ShippingLine](https://shopify.dev/docs/api/admin-graphql/latest/objects/ShippingLine)). Add a `refund_items` table (refund, order item, quantity, net refunded, discount reversed, tax refunded, both currencies), a `refund_taxes` table (refund, the original `order_taxes` row, refunded base and invoiced amount) so each rate and each shipping portion is reversed exactly, and a shipping part on the refund (net and tax refunded, with the original shipping tax rows referenced). Add `base_amount` to `refunds`, fix the refundable cap so shipping can be refunded, and drive order status from cumulative refunded versus paid. Add a nullable per-line counter such as refunded quantity on `order_items` or derive it from `refund_items`. The reason: refunds are the one flow where the stored order must be run backwards, and each missing link forces an estimate that will not reconcile to an invoice.

## Missing net bases make exact recomputation and audit impossible

The schema stores tax amounts and rates but not the numbers they were computed from. The notes establish that no column holds net shipping, the shipping discount, the per-line allocated net shipping, or the per-line taxable base (code: `CreateOrdersTable.php:47-56`, `CreateOrderItemsTable.php`). Net shipping can be derived only as `shipping_total - shipping_tax_amount`, and the shipping discount only from a free-shipping coupon row. The allocated EU net portion per line is recoverable only as `tax * 100 / rate`, which is lossy under HALF_UP and undefined at 0%. The allocation weights (post-discount taxable amount, gross under inclusive pricing, net under exclusive) cannot be replayed after the fact. Zero-portion lines write no shipping row, and a fully discounted cart falls back to one unattributed row, so the same order shape can be per-item or unattributed and consumers must handle both (code: `EUTaxStrategy.php:104-117`). The same missing base blocks a net-sales-by-rate report for shipping and for items.

The reference platforms store the base alongside the tax. Shopify attaches tax lines and discount allocations to both product lines and shipping lines; Stripe carries a separate shipping cost with its own tax behaviour and discount ([Shopify DiscountAllocation](https://shopify.dev/docs/api/admin-graphql/latest/objects/DiscountAllocation), [Stripe custom tax](https://docs.stripe.com/tax/custom)). The suggested structure has three parts. Add a taxable base column to `order_taxes` (base and invoiced), so every row records base, rate and tax and the Art. 226 grouping by rate is a simple sum. Add a shipping net, shipping discount and net-shipping-after-discount figure to `orders` (or, better, move shipping into its own `order_shipping_lines` table with method snapshot, original price, discount, net, tax and tax-inclusion, mirroring Shopify and Woo shipping lines). Add an allocated shipping net per line, either on `order_items` or in a small allocation table. Reason: with the base stored, rounding, reports and refunds all become reads instead of reconstructions, and a shipping line table also fixes the thin `shipping_metadata` snapshot, which holds only method id, name and type and no cost or taxable flag (code: `CreateOrderAction.php:~852-870`).

## Order edits and tax-inclusive pricing rewrite history

Edits are a high-severity gap. `UpdateOrderAction` deletes and recreates `order_taxes` and `order_coupons` from the current settings, so rate history follows whatever the store settings are at edit time, and `orders.is_tax_inclusive` is overwritten with the current store flag (code: `PersistsOrderTaxes.php:47-80`, `PersistsOrderCoupons.php:64, 88`, `UpdateOrderAction.php:130-139, 261`). The coupon snapshot is refreshed from the live coupon, which contradicts the spec's "immutable after checkout" promise for order edits (it holds only for coupon edits). `DiscountService` drops coupons that are no longer valid, so editing an old order can silently remove an expired coupon; the notes did not verify whether `UpdateOrderAction` revalidates or reuses stored coupons. Per-line attribution is keyed by `variant_id`, so a deleted variant (null `variant_id`) cannot be matched on edit, and two lines of the same variant would collide; the notes did not verify whether the cart can hold duplicate-variant lines. The suggested structure is an explicit order-revision or adjustment model: keep the original checkout snapshot rows immutable, and record edits as a new revision (or as signed adjustment lines) with its own tax and coupon rows, a `revision` identifier, and the tax settings snapshot used. At minimum, freeze `is_tax_inclusive` and the rate used, and change attribution keys from `variant_id` to `order_item_id`. Reason: an invoice already issued must not change when a merchant edits a tax setting.

Tax-inclusive pricing is stored inconsistently. Item price and subtotal are converted to net, but `base_discount_amount` and the coupon attributions stay on the discount engine's basis, which the code implies is gross but the notes did not verify independently (code: `RecalculateCartAction.php:388-401`, `PersistsOrderCoupons.php:119-120`). So `subtotal - discount` is not the taxable base in inclusive mode, and the gross customer-facing unit price is not stored; it must be rebuilt from net, tax and quantity through a float tax fraction that can differ by a minor unit (code: `RecalculateCartAction.php:417-446`). Shipping is always added on top as exclusive, even in inclusive stores (code: `AbstractTaxStrategy.php:80-94`), which is acceptable but must be documented. Magento's `price_incl_tax`, `row_total_incl_tax` and `discount_tax_compensation_amount` show the usual fix ([Magento StackExchange](https://backiee.wasmer.app/https_magento_stackexchange_com/a/363761)): store both net and gross unit price and row total, store the discount in both net and gross, and store the tax-inclusion flag per order line and shipping line so a mixed or changed setting never reinterprets old rows. The same gap blocks computing the tax saved by each coupon.

## Rounding, rate provenance and smaller defects

Rounding is per line with HALF_UP, in base currency, with no order-level adjustment step, then each `invoiced_*` column is converted and rounded independently (code: `AbstractTaxStrategy.php:68-77`, `CreateOrderAction.php:~812-819`). Only `sum(base_amount)` of tax rows is asserted against the order tax total; invoiced sums are not checked and can drift by a few minor units, and coupon invoiced shares reconcile only within one minor unit per coupon (code: `PersistsOrderTaxes.php:119-132`, `PersistsOrderCoupons.php:169-194`). EU law sets no harmonised rounding level and leaves the method to Member States ([VATupdate on C-302/07](https://www.vatupdate.com/2021/08/25/flashback-on-ecj-cases-c-302-07-j-d-wetherspoon-rules-on-rounding-of-vat-amounts/)); the notes cite an unverified vendor claim that some countries, such as Italy, require document-level calculation ([Thomson Reuters](https://nttdata.hostedtax.thomsonreuters.com/determination-help/Content/uiguide/concepts/rounding.htm)). Because Art. 226 anchors the invoice on VAT per rate, a line-rounded store can disagree with a per-rate invoice total by cents. The suggested fix is a stored taxable base per rate (above), an explicit rounding-adjustment column or row per order, a configured rounding level per tax region, and converting invoiced figures from the summed base once, allocating the remainder, instead of per column. Note the notes did not quantify float effects in the inclusive net derivation.

Rate provenance is missing. `order_taxes` has a free-text `name` and a rate but no country or jurisdiction, no tax-profile or rule reference, no tax code and no compound or priority flag. The cause chain is that a profile is only a label and the real rate sits in a settings option and decision rules (code: `CreateTaxProfilesTable.php:27-31`, `TaxStrategyFactory.php:62-93`), so after the fact the order cannot say which rule produced a rate. OSS reporting needs the country of consumption, which today must be read from `orders.shipping_country`, and the strategy is chosen from the shipping country only, so billing-country-based supplies cannot be modelled (code: `TaxStrategyFactory.php:33-43`). Add `jurisdiction_country` (and state), `tax_code`, `tax_profile_id` and rule reference as nullable snapshot columns, plus a compound flag only if compound taxes become a requirement (none exists today). WooCommerce keys tax arrays to a mutable rate table, which the notes judge less audit-safe than storing rate and label on the order ([WooCommerce wiki](https://github.com/woocommerce/woocommerce/wiki/Order-and-Order-Line-Item-Data)).

Coupon-side gaps are medium to low. The tax effect per coupon is not recorded. The shipping discount has no column; the read path derives it only for coupons whose `discount_target === 'order'` (code: `OrderResource.php:654-677`). The notes flag a probable defect: free-shipping coupons usually save `discount_target = NULL` (frontend nulls it for non-amount-off coupons, backend requires it only for amount-off), so `shipping_strikethrough` may omit a free-shipping waiver (code: `coupon-form.ts:160`, `CouponCreateRequest.php:57`). The notes did not confirm this with a real saved coupon. Add a shipping-discount column on `order_coupons` and read `discount_type` instead of `discount_target`. Add a unique key on (`order_item_id`, `order_coupon_id`) to `order_item_coupons`, store the pre-clamp intended amount if you need to explain clamped discounts, and note that buy-x-get-y is a no-op that writes a zero-amount row, with no column for free-unit quantity (code: `DiscountService.php:395-405`). The notes also did not confirm whether `coupon_snapshot` includes the eligible product and category relations. Fixed product-coupon semantics are per line, not per unit or per cart (code: `DiscountService.php:491-502`), which must be documented because merchants will assume otherwise.

## Behaviour defects and unverified items outside the tables

Several gaps are code behaviour, not columns. `variants.charge_taxes` is stored but never read, so non-taxable variants are still taxed (code: grep of `app/`). `is_shipping_tax_enabled` is hard-coded true in `TaxStrategyFactory.php:45-46`. `reverse_charge`, `tax_id_number` and `tax_id_type` exist on `orders` but nothing sets or reads `reverse_charge`. EU regions have no independent shipping rate, so "EU shipping at a flat 19% regardless of goods" is impossible; that matches your distribution requirement but removes any merchant choice of a single flat rate, and the notes found no support for Germany or Austria special rules or a main-item rule. Model casts are inconsistent: `Order` casts only tax and fee columns to int, `OrderItem` casts no money columns, so uncast values return as strings. `refunds.created_by` references `users.id` while `orders.created_by` references `users.ID`, `RefundStatus` omits the `failed` status that the migration comment lists, and there is no unique key on `orders.order_number` or `uuid`. Unverified in the notes: whether a later schema-upgrade migration adds `products.tax_profile_id` (the model uses it but the products migration lacks it; only `database/` was grepped), whether `product_data` snapshots the tax profile, whether `find_by_codes` preserves cart attach order (order-wide percentage coupons are order-dependent), the `Money::convert_to_currency` rounding mode, whether `UpdateOrderAction` recomputes `exchange_rate`, and `docs/ecommerce/settings/tax.yml`, which looks stale against the strategy code.

## Conclusion

The schema is a sound checkout snapshot, not a ledger. It correctly records what was charged, at which rate, on which line, including EU per-product shipping tax and stacked coupon shares, but it records outputs without the inputs that produced them and treats the order as rebuildable, which is the opposite of what refunds, VAT returns and issued invoices need. The shortest path to fit is not a redesign of the existing tables: add the taxable base and provenance to `order_taxes`, give shipping its own line table, freeze the checkout snapshot behind a revision model, and add refund-line and refund-tax tables. Those four changes close the critical and high gaps; the rest are incremental columns and code fixes.

Open questions that need a decision before building: whether EU shipping must always follow goods or merchants may choose a flat rate (the notes found no primary national text mandating the pro-rata split), which rounding level each target country requires, and whether the unverified `products.tax_profile_id` and free-shipping `discount_target` findings hold on a real installation.
